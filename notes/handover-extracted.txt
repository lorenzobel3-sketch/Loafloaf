1. The brand premise
THE SOURDOUGH HOUSE sells one loaf made of four things: flour, water, salt, and time.
Three of them cost pennies. The fourth is the entire business. Every section teaches that
one idea, that what you pay for is the thirty-six hours nobody else will give the bread. The
interactive moment makes the visitor perform the wait. The closing line collects on it. If a
section does not serve the four-ingredient idea, it does not ship.
Register: warm, plain, homely, quietly confident. A baker talking across a counter, not a
brand talking at a market.
2. Palette as CSS tokens
Direction sampled from the reference photo's world (flour linen, crochet lace, weathered
wood, blistered crust, blown-out window light). EXACT VALUES FINALIZED FROM
APPROVED FOOTAGE AFTER THE VIDEO GATE.
:root{
  --canvas:#EDE6D8;        /* flour-dusted linen, never pure white */
  --panel:#E3D9C6;         /* oat, the shadow inside the crochet */
  --accent:#7B3B1B;        /* the deep caramelised ridge of the score */
  --accent-hover:#94502A;
  --accent-muted:rgba(123,59,27,.18);  /* borders, glows, particles */
  --text-secondary:#6E6152;

}
Design-bar deviation, said out loud: warm cream canvas is on the banned AI-cliche list.
Taking the carve-out because cream, linen and stoneware ARE this subject's own material
world. Earning it three ways: tones sampled from the actual footage, NOT terracotta as the
accent (burnt crust ridge instead), and no stock bakery template layout. No two adjacent
sections share a skeleton.

SIGNATURE: the score.
 One hand-drawn SVG curve, the lame's slash. It is the logo
mark, every section divider, and the rule under the settle headline. Dividers draw
